## The raccoon pose: it does not work. Round 03 must explore new poses.
I'll say it plainly: the port-arms diagonal has failed in practice, and polishing it won't fix it.
- **It covers the face.** The barrel crosses the outer end of the mask and the near cheek ruff on the screen-left
  side of the head (a close-up shows the red tube over the mask's dark lobe from about hero (320,380) to (480,520)).
  The hero's best-drawn area is the one being hidden.
- **It splits the figure in two** along a diagonal, so the torso turns into two leftover triangles.
- **It stacks clutter in the middle.** Both hands, the gauge, the scarf knot and the bottom cap all meet in one
  60 x 60 px area under the chin.
- **The bottom cap plus the foot plate** read as a second crossing bar (a "+" at the lower right), not as the foot
  of a pump.
- **The hose curls up into an umbrella-handle hook** with the chuck pointing at the sky.
- **The tail** rises at the left and ends in a dark notched blob that reads as a second, waving paw.
- **All three variants are this one pose,** so it is no longer exploration.

**Round 03: three genuinely different poses, all with the face 100% clear.** Same head (keep it), different body:

1. **Bat on the shoulder.** The near paw grips the barrel at chest height, and the pump rests on the near shoulder
   and runs up and back **behind** the head toward the upper left. The T-handle peeks out behind the far side of the
   head or above the near ear. The face is untouched, the diagonal sits behind the figure, and the attitude is
   casual swagger. The other paw is on the hip or thumb-hooked in the bandana.
2. **The cane pose (gentleman villain).** The pump is planted vertically in front of the belly, its foot on the
   bottom edge of the frame (a natural finished bottom), and **both paws are stacked on the T-handle** at chest
   height, just below the chin. The face sits clear above the hands, the hands become a large, readable feature
   (finally enough space to draw real fingers), and the silhouette is compact and calm. Smug, chin slightly up,
   eyes sliding right toward Dog Fjord.
3. **The elbow lean.** The pump is planted vertically at screen-left, with the T-handle at shoulder height. The
   raccoon leans its near elbow on the handle, the paw dangling loose over it, and the body tilts toward the pump.
   The far arm crosses over or rests on the hip. This is A's planted staff from round 01 with real attitude and real
   contact.

(One more if there's time: **pointing the pump at Dog Fjord like a sword** at arm's length, low, *below* the snout
line, so it is a gesture toward the opponent.)

In all three: the hose hangs with gravity (a slack U or S loop down from the foot, with the chuck lying or dangling,
never hooked upward). The tail is either hidden or shown as a tufted tip curling round the hip with a light-tipped
ring, never a dark knob.

## Accents, ranked
1. **Red bandana + teal pump (b).** This is the best harmony. The hottest colour (red) sits right under the chin and
   frames the face, so it pulls the eye *to* the hero (exactly what ref 04 does). The teal pump is the complement of
   the warm taupe coat and the amber eyes, and being cool it recedes instead of competing with the face. It also
   keeps a clear visual link to bike share.
2. **Green scarf + red pump (a).** It reads well and the scarf is the ref-03 nod, but the most saturated colour on the
   figure is now the pump, so the eye goes to the prop. Red and green at equal saturation also verges on "Christmas".
   If you keep it, take the pump down to a deep oxblood or cherry-maroon and let the scarf be the dominant accent.
3. **Cycling cap + chrome pump (c).** The cap is a lovely character note (the rounder crown, the 3-tone flipped
   brim and its shadow on the brow are well done). But together with the chrome pump the picture loses its colour
   focus: white cap, grey pump, grey-taupe fur, and the only saturated spot is the brim at the top of the head. Keep
   the cap as an optional accessory (it would work with the teal pump plus a small red element), not as this set.

## Dog Fjord: what is still missing
1. **He reads as a pig, not a bulldog.** It is a smooth egg with pointed side flaps, a pink snout zone and a curved
   grin. Push the bulldog cues:
   - **Underbite:** the lower jaw slightly in front, with a row of lower teeth or **two little lower canines poking up
     over the upper lip** at the mouth corners. This one cue says "bulldog" more than anything else.
   - **Flews that hang past the jaw line:** today the lobes sit *inside* the head outline. Let them droop below the
     mouth corners and break the lower silhouette on both sides, with a dark crease under each lobe.
   - **A wide, flat, square jaw:** widen the lower third so the head is broader at the jowls than at the brow (a pear
     upside down, as in iter_04's broad base), with a flat chin plane.
   - **A nose rope:** a thick wrinkle roll arching over the top of the nose, plus a deep stop crease between the eyes.
   - **Rose ears:** keep iter_04's placement (low at the upper sides), but draw them folded back. Show the fold
     crease and a sliver of the darker inner ear, with a rounded tip, instead of the stiff pointed triangle that
     reads as pig.
   - Optional: a cream-white muzzle or chin blaze inside the pink (many bulldogs have one). It breaks up the pink
     field and is instantly canine.
2. **The smugness is gone.** In a, the patch eye is wide awake: the lid covers only the top third, the whole iris
   shows and the lower lid is lit, so it reads friendly and alert. iter_09's heavy-lidded patch eye was the villain.
   Bring it back: the upper lid down to about the centre of the pupil, the iris sliding **sideways toward the raccoon
   (screen-left)**, the patch brow arched high over a lowered lid (raised brow plus low lid = smug), the mouth
   corner on the patch side higher. Keep the wink on the other eye for `grin`. c's lid is closest. Copy that.
3. **Chin up (b) is the right idea, executed too timidly.** To read as "looking down his nose" it needs the nostrils
   visible from below (the underside of the nose shown), the pupils *low* under heavy lids, the neck or double chin
   showing as a distinct mass, and the grin seen from below (the curve flatter). Try it in one round-03 variant with
   c's sunk shoulders as the alternative.
4. **The rendering.** The modelling is now real (lit flew tops with core shadows, the dome highlight, folds as shadow
   crescents with lit ridges). Remaining:
   - The nasolabial folds are still thin dark arcs on the far side (the curve from the nose wing round the grin
     corner reads as a line at k = 1).
   - The teeth are a flat white band with tick separations; give the corners a shadow and add the lower teeth.
   - b's skin speckle: keep a's texture density.
5. **Bust:** take **c's sunk, hunched broad shoulders** (the head resting into the suit, the jowls on the lapels).
   For the tie, either a's **gold tie with the little red cars** (the best satire) or b's red tie on a charcoal
   pinstripe. The flag pin is fine.

## Top 5 fixes for round 03 (the last exploration round)
1. **Raccoon: three new poses with the face completely clear.** Bat on the shoulder behind the head, the cane pose
   with both paws stacked on a planted pump, and the elbow lean on a planted pump. Drop the port-arms diagonal. Use
   the bandana + teal pump set as the default accent, and the cap on one of the three.
2. **Dog Fjord: make him a bulldog.** An underbite with two lower canines over the lip, flews hanging past the jaw
   line, a wide flat jaw (broader at the jowls than the brow), a nose rope, and rose ears folded back in iter_04's
   position. Use c's sunk-into-the-shoulders posture as the base, and try b's chin-up properly in one variant.
3. **Dog Fjord: give him his smugness back.** A heavy-lidded patch eye with the lid at the pupil centre and the iris
   sliding toward the raccoon, the patch brow arched high, and the patch-side mouth corner raised.
4. **Raccoon below the head: simplify and enlarge.**
   - Replace the torn-paper chest patches (the jagged dark-taupe islands on the chest's shadow side) with 2 or 3 big
     tufted tiers that follow the chest's form.
   - Make the hands bigger, with a lighter knuckle value and palm pad so they read against the dark arm.
   - Remove the dark notched tail tip (hide the tail or give it a light tufted tip).
5. **Pump and hose as believable objects.** The hose hangs in a slack gravity loop (no upward hook). The foot plate
   reads as a foot (for a planted pump, flat on the bottom edge with two pegs). The gauge dial faces the viewer and
   sits where no hand covers it. Keep `pumpAxis`.
