1. **The gum bar: landed.** The far lip edge is now a short tapered sliver. It follows the upper-lip line and rises to the far corner, and it reads as the lip's lower edge in the grin and the laugh. It is correctly absent in the shock.

## 4. Closing verdict

**Against the baseline (iter_00).**
- **Dog Fjord:** a different character in the best sense. The baseline's 180-wide onion head, with features spread out and horn-like ears, is now a ~138-wide bulldog head with the features clustered and jowl weight.
  - The face went from generic cheer to the smug politician the 3D had: a wink, a cocked brow, a heavy-lidded patch eye, a lopsided grin with drooping lip lines.
  - The thin hairlines and dither bands are gone. Everything is flat 3-tone with clean outlines.
- **Raccoon:** the profile-snout head on a sack body with abs is now a 3/4 head with a two-lobe mask, amber eyes with glints, a lowered snout and a one-sided smirk. It sits on a chibi bust with chunky outlined arms in a hand-on-hip pose and a ringed tail.

**Against the old 3D row.**
- **Proportions:**
  - The dog matches on purpose: ~138 wide (the 3D ×1.1, chosen for the 112x100 frame), ~148 tall against the 3D's ~144, and the eye, nose and mouth spacing taken from the 3D.
  - The raccoon's head matches the 3D (~108 against ~103, centred over the body). The body is deliberately smaller and chunkier than the 3D's, following refs 4/5 and the in-frame rule.
- **Symmetry/structure:** both are now consistent 3/4 views with features placed on the 3D's axes. The dog is slightly more frontal than the 3D, and its top is a touch narrower than the 3D's broad skull.
- **Overall shapes:**
  - The dog's silhouette reads like the 3D's: broad rounded head, corner ears folded outward, pompadour hugging the crown, U-shaped jowls.
  - The 3D's sculpted muzzle volume is only *suggested* in 2D, through the nose shadow, a single fold and the lip edge. This is the biggest remaining gap.
  - The raccoon keeps the 3D's head shape and swaps the blobby torso for clear tube forms. That swap is better than the 3D.
- **2D character:** both exceed the 3D. Crisp outlines, flat cel shadows on one side, no blobby gradients, readable eyes with highlights, and fingers and knuckles that read by silhouette. Both survive small sizes. The dog's wink, lid and grin read in the 112x100 dialogue portrait at 1x and 2x.

**Known limits (not blockers):**
- The dog's muzzle could project more.
- At 1x the wink brow and the wink arc nearly touch.
- The raccoon's face is fine, but less punchy than ref 4's open grin. That is by design: the brief asks for a smirk.

**Ship both.**
